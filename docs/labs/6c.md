## 6C-1 · The Energy Chain Bench — `g6c-energy-chain` (C1)

Set-ups (6): `forms` C1.1 · `kinetic` C1.2 · `potential` C1.3 · `chain` C1.4 + C1.1 ·
`conserve` C1.5 · `everyday` C1.6 + C1.4. 34 controls (shown by set-up/device), 17 presets,
7 problems, 7 walkthrough steps, 5 quiz questions.

**Model.** The cart is integrated along the track's arc length (midpoint step, 0.5 ms): gravity,
rolling resistance μN with N including the curve's centripetal part, drag ½ρC_dAv², eddy brake bv,
clay yield 60 N. Light gates time a flag (v = flag/Δt). The chain steps sunlight → panel →
battery → motor (3 W) → pulley → mass → generator → lamp with real efficiencies. Kettle: lumped
water + 550 g body, side losses 1.6 W/K, evaporation by mass transfer (h_m 1 cm/s). Bike: constant
brake deceleration + drag + rolling. Phone: CC to 80 % then taper, charger η, cable I²R.

**Numbers reproduced** (all in `ms/g6c.test.ts`):
- ½kx² = 0.50 J → √(2E/m) = 1.41 m/s; the gate reads 1.389 m/s (losses ≈ 3 %).
- v = √(2gh): 2.426 m/s from 0.30 m (gate 2.400), independent of mass; Moon 0.986 m/s (gate 0.982).
- Ledger KE + PE + spring + thermal = start to < 0.1 % in every case (after the fix below, to 1e-5).
- Chain: 0.21·0.95·0.78·0.9·0.9·0.78·0.35 = 3.44 %; in = stored + light + heat.
- Kettle 1 L, 20→100 °C, 2 kW: mcΔT/P = 167 s; model 185 s (≈ 3 min, 91 %).
- Bike 80 kg at 8 m/s: 2560 J; disc rotor +35.5 K.

**Bugs found.** (1) Explicit Euler drifted the valley ledger by +3.5 % over 30 s — replaced by a
midpoint step; ledger now exact. (2) First kettle evaporation term was dimensionally wrong (105 kJ
with the lid on) — rewritten as vapour-density mass transfer. (3) Heights were offset by the
track's deck height in two readouts and the equation (start mgh, ledger) — removed. (4) Leaked
`textAlign` from R3 labels centred the card titles. (5) Camera did not re-home when the device
changed.

**Upgrade pass 1** (after it worked): slow-motion (0.4×) looping launches so the cart is seen
moving; gate-to-gate average speed as a second measurement method; every turn-round in the valley
marked on the track with its height; per-device camera homes; opaque cards; Sankey labels fitted.
**Upgrade pass 2** (after the contact sheets): chain bench recomposed (lower stand, leads routed
along the bench and up the rod, the panel actually turns with the tilt, the floodlight stays
aimed); forms bench compacted and its callouts separated; phone charger plugged into a wall socket;
bicycle camera pulled back; phone layouts re-centred when the card folds into a chip.

## 6C-2 · The Particle Box — `g6c-particle-box` (C2)

Set-ups (6): `particles` C2.1 · `brownian` C2.2 · `phases` C2.3 · `temperature` C2.4 · `total` C2.5 ·
`thermometer` C2.6. 25 controls (shown by set-up), 16 presets, 6 problems, 7 walkthrough steps,
5 quiz questions.

**Model.** 2D Lennard-Jones molecular dynamics (velocity Verlet, cell list, 2.5σ shifted cut-off,
WCA-type walls, gentle gravity, Andersen heat bath or a constant-power heater); phase measured from
neighbours within 1.5σ and the mean-square distance strayed in a 1.6τ window. Mixtures in argon's
units with real σ, ε, m ratios (He, Ne, Ar, Xe). Syringe: Boyle with an adiabatic first squeeze
relaxing (τ 18 s), bulk moduli for liquids. Beads: Stokes–Einstein, Vogel viscosity, 300 seeded
random walks at 30 frames/s. Ice calorimeter: Q = mcΔT, 334 J/g. Thermometer: two lumped heat
capacities and the sample's loss to the room; IR gun by emissivity.

**Numbers reproduced** (`ms/g6c.test.ts`): NVE energy drift < 0.5 % over 1500 steps; 40 mL air
under 4 kg → 25.3 mL (Boyle); water shrinks 1.08 µL (K = 2.2 GPa); D = 0.429 µm²/s for a 1 µm bead
in water; Perrin's N_A from the tracks 5.95 × 10²³ (true 6.022); tea 250 g at 70 °C melts 219 g of
ice; v_rms 1368 m/s (He) and 239 m/s (Xe) at 300 K; glass thermometer in 1 mL at 80 °C settles at
66.6 °C.

**Bugs found.** (1) The hexagonal start lattice put two atoms 0.56σ apart at the end of odd rows →
forces of 10⁵ and an exploding box; rows now end inside the wall. (2) First wall potential
diverged for a particle that crossed it; clamped. (3) The piston barostat converged too slowly to
measure Boyle's law in MD — replaced, for C2.1, by a real syringe on real equations of state, with
the MD as the magnified view. (4) Raw argon ε put the 2D model's triple point at 49 K, so "argon
at 70 K" was a liquid; ε is now set so the model melts at the real melting point (stated in the
eqNote; boiling is not calibrated). (5) The object select's defaults made the temperature sweep
non-deterministic (live.mjs caught it); the object a temperature and mass belong to is now a
parameter. (6) A tiny stage during layout gave negative gradient radii; sizes are floored.

**Upgrade pass 1:** three tagged atoms leave trails (a scribble in a solid, a wander in a liquid);
a card shows the two measures that decide the phase against their thresholds; the ice jar became
glass; the meltwater drains into a cylinder sized to the job; card labels clear their stage plates.
**Upgrade pass 2** (after the sheets): the Brownian inset now holds one big particle in the middle
with its own trail among the molecules; the syringe camera re-framed to keep the load in view; the
thermometer column's reading moved clear of the melting/boiling marks.

## 6C-3 · The Heat Transfer Bench — `g6c-heat-transfer` (C3)

Set-ups (6): `direction` C3.1 · `conduction` C3.2 · `convection` C3.3 · `radiation` C3.4 ·
`materials` C3.5 · `equilibrium` C3.6. 31 controls (shown by set-up), 15 presets, 6 problems,
7 walkthrough steps, 5 quiz questions. Thermal-camera view on three set-ups.

**Model.** Two lumped blocks with a contact conductance and losses; Ingen-Housz's rods by the 1D
fin equation (explicit, 121 nodes, insulated far end) for seven real materials; a 2D Boussinesq
tank (vorticity–stream function, SOR Poisson, upwind advection, an eddy viscosity for the 6 mm
grid, sub-stepped for stability) with advected dye; Leslie's cube by εσ(T⁴ − T₀⁴) and an on-axis
disc view factor to a thermopile; contact temperature by effusivity; the ice-melting blocks by 1D
conduction through the block and a 0.1 mm meltwater film; the method of mixtures with a leaky cup.

**Numbers reproduced** (`ms/g6c.test.ts`): blocks meet at 59.0 °C (Al 0.5 kg at 80, Cu 0.5 kg at
10); copper melt length 16.7 cm (fin formula) and the rods in the order of k with
(x_Cu/x_steel)² = 27 against k ratio 25 (finite rod); heating from below turns the tank over,
from above it stratifies; Leslie's cube at 90 °C: black 5.73 mV, white 5.49, polished 0.30; skin
on Al 20.6 °C, on wood 30.2 °C; ice melts on Al in ~95 s, >10× longer on acrylic; 100 g at 80 °C
+ 100 g at 20 °C → 50.2 °C with lost = gained + leaked.

**Bugs found.** (1) 30 cm rods let copper melt to the end (finite-fin effect) — 60 cm rods and an
interpolated melt front recover Ingen-Housz's ratio. (2) Ice melted on aluminium in 5 s: no
contact resistance; a meltwater film added. (3) The glycerol tank went to NaN: explicit diffusion
past dx²/4ν — the step now sub-divides itself. (4) The animation clock survived setup(), so the
thermal-camera toggle looked non-deterministic to live.mjs — reset in setup.

**Upgrade pass 1:** cameras re-framed per set-up (blocks close, rods from above, Leslie's cube from
the side so the detector does not hide it); the wax coat tinted by the rod's own metal so the
materials stay readable; the heat-flux meter moved clear of the blocks; tank shown with dye and
warmth together by default. **Upgrade pass 2** (after the sheets): the "both" tank view tints the
water by temperature instead of the dark camera palette; the thermopile's meter moved out from
behind the detector.
